How to check a Base token yourself, without a scanner

No account, no API key, no score to trust. Five calls answer most of what matters, and you can run them again next month.

1. Does the contract exist, and what shape is it

curl -s https://mainnet.base.org -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{
  "jsonrpc":"2.0","id":1,"method":"eth_getCode",
  "params":["0xYOUR_TOKEN","latest"]}' | jq -r .result | wc -c

Empty bytecode is not a token. Very small bytecode is usually a proxy: the logic lives at another address and the address you were given can be repointed. When a report says "upgradeable", this is the call that showed it.

2. Who is the owner

curl -s https://mainnet.base.org -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{
  "jsonrpc":"2.0","id":1,"method":"eth_call",
  "params":[{"to":"0xYOUR_TOKEN","data":"0x8da5cb5b"},"latest"]}'

0x8da5cb5b is owner(). No result means the contract never declared an owner, which is not the same as safe: a custom admin slot may hold the same power.

3. Is the owner a wallet or another contract

curl -s https://mainnet.base.org -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{
  "jsonrpc":"2.0","id":1,"method":"eth_getCode","params":["0xOWNER","latest"]}'

0x means one key. Anything else is code, often a multisig — better, but only if you know who the signers are, and the chain alone will not tell you that.

4. Can the supply still change

curl -s https://mainnet.base.org -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{
  "jsonrpc":"2.0","id":1,"method":"eth_call",
  "params":[{"to":"0xYOUR_TOKEN","data":"0x18160ddd"},"latest"]}'

0x18160ddd is totalSupply(). Read it twice, a day apart. If it grew without a burn, somebody can mint — whatever the code claims.

5. What none of this tells you

QuestionWhy the chain alone cannot answer it
Is it a honeypot?Needs a real buy and a real sell, with real money
What is the transfer tax?Measured by moving tokens, not by looking at them
Is liquidity locked?Needs the LP holder list and the unlock contract
Will the price rise?Nobody knows. Anyone certain is selling something

A number with no block and no method is a request for trust. These five calls take five minutes and leave you the receipts.
