Scanner score vs evidence-based audit: what the difference costs you

Both are useful. They answer different questions, and confusing them is how people lose money.

What a free scanner does well

  • runs a buy and a sell and reports the tax actually taken
  • counts holders and flags concentration
  • answers in milliseconds, for any address, at no cost

That is real work and worth using. The case studies quote the free scanner's answer next to ours for the same address, unedited.

What a score cannot do

ProblemWhy
You cannot re-check itA score is a verdict with no block: when it changes you cannot tell whether the token changed or the model did
It hides what it skippedA green badge never says "honeypot untested, pool too thin"
It cannot be argued withNo method, no diff, no hash: nothing to point at

What an evidence-based audit gives you

  • the block every value was read at
  • the exact calls, so you can repeat them
  • a sha256, so you can prove what you were shown
  • a list of what was not established — the part sales pages usually drop

How to use both

scanner   -> is there an obvious trap right now?      (fast, free, opaque)
evidence  -> what does the chain say, and can I prove it later?  (slower, checkable)

Run the free scanner first. If it flags a honeypot, stop and keep your money. If it is green, that is exactly when the unread fields matter — and they are the column a scanner never prints.
